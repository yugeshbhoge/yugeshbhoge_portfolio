Constraining Neutron Star Equation of State

Bayesian model selection across 900 simulated and 3 real NSBH mergers, from today's LIGO network to the Einstein Telescope and Cosmic Explorer.

Vasudev Dubey · Rahul Kashyap · Yugesh Bhoge
Department of Physics, IIT Bombay

Motivation

The internal structure of Neutron Stars (NS) is still one of the central open problems in astrophysics. Only a handful of Binary Neutron Star (BNS: GW170817, GW190425) and Neutron Star-Black Hole (NSBH) mergers (GW200105, GW200115, GW230529) have been observed so far, far too few to tightly pin down the Equation of State (EoS).

Because black holes carry exactly zero tidal deformability, NSBH mergers isolate the neutron star's own tidal signature far more cleanly than BNS events, where the two stars' effects are entangled into a single combined parameter. With next-generation detectors; O5, A♯, and 3G facilities like the Einstein Telescope and Cosmic Explorer, expected to push annual NSBH detection rates far higher, the open question becomes: how many events are actually needed to distinguish one EoS from another?

This work extends a companion Bayesian model-selection study of BNS systems (Kashyap et al., 2025). Consistent with that earlier work, BNS events remain more efficient at constraining the EoS per detection, but NSBH-based inference reaches comparable confidence given a moderately larger number of events.

Theoretical Framework

1. Dimensionless Tidal Deformability

$$ \Lambda = \frac{2}{3} k_2 \left( \frac{c^2 R}{G M} \right)^5 $$

Significance: $\Lambda$ quantifies the "stiffness" of the star, built from the, $l=2$ tidal Love number $k_2$ and compactness $R/M$. A stiffer EoS yields a larger radius and a much larger $\Lambda$. Because NSBH mergers involve only one deformable body, they let us recover a neutron star's bare $\Lambda$ directly, rather than the combined $\tilde{\Lambda}$ measured in BNS mergers.

2. Cumulative Log-Odds Ratio (N events)

$$ \log \mathcal{O}^i_j = (N-1)\log f + \sum_{n=1}^{N} \left[ \log \mathcal{Z}(H_i|d_n) - \log \mathcal{Z}(H_j|d_n) \right] $$

Significance: Combines the per-event Bayesian evidence $\mathcal{Z}$ from $N$ NSBH detections into a single odds ratio favouring EoS hypothesis $H_i$ over $H_j$. The correction factor $f$ accounts for the fact that different EoS models imply different maximum NS masses, and therefore different prior volumes, which would otherwise bias the comparison.

Methodology

  • Injection: Synthetic signals generated with IMRPhenomNSBH (cross-checked against SEOBNRv4_ROM_NRTidalv2_NSBH) for three injected EoS; APR4, SLy, DD2, recovered against 11 candidate models spanning nucleonic, hyperonic, phase-transition, and piecewise-polytropic classes.
  • Population: 900 simulated NSBH events (100 per EoS × detector-network pair) across three configurations, O5, A♯, and a 3G network (Einstein Telescope + Cosmic Explorer, labelled ECC), with full 17-dimensional Bayesian parameter estimation per event.
  • Sampler: Utilized the Bilby library with relative-binning likelihoods. dynesty and nessai were benchmarked head-to-head; nessai was adopted for all final runs after showing 3.8× to 30.5× faster sampling with statistically consistent evidence.
  • Evidence & validation: Computed in two 2D subspaces, $(\tilde\Lambda,\eta)$ and $(m_2,\Lambda_2)$, then combined across events via the cumulative log-odds ratio above. The same pipeline was applied to public GWOSC posterior samples for three real LVK NSBH detections.

Analysis

Across all three injected EoS and detector networks, the true EoS consistently achieves the highest Bayesian evidence, and the recovered $\Lambda_2$ vs. NS-mass relation encloses the injected curve at 1σ regardless of which waveform model is used. Discriminating power is strongest in the $(m_2,\Lambda_2)$ subspace and grows sharply with sensitivity: moving from O5 to a 3G (ECC) network roughly quadruples the achievable log-evidence separation over 50 events.

Recovery becomes markedly less certain and less discriminating, for higher-mass neutron stars ($\gtrsim 2\,M_\odot$), where $\Lambda_2 \to 0$ and the measurement error approaches the size of the signal itself.

Applications

This Bayesian model-selection framework is designed to scale with LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA's O5 and A♯ upgrades, and thanks to the sampler speed-up demonstrated here remains computationally tractable for next-generation networks like the Einstein Telescope and Cosmic Explorer, where NSBH detection rates are expected to rise dramatically.

Implications

Because NSBH mergers isolate a single neutron star's tidal response, they offer a clean window into the nuclear EoS. Ranking 11 candidate models such as nucleonic, hyperonic, phase-transition, and piecewise-polytropic against real and simulated data helps narrow the allowed nuclear symmetry energy and feeds directly into the broader QCD phase diagram at supranuclear densities.
